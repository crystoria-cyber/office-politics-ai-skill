# Research Protocol: How CPS Should Study Real Corporate Cases

## Goal
Build a reliable, searchable evidence base for analyzing organizational power and political behavior, including Indian promoter-led companies, family succession, informal SMEs, employee factions, HR decisions, labour relations, workplace safety, harassment, whistleblowing and governance.

## 1. Search in layers

1. **Define the question.** Is the case about board power, owner/family influence, employee-level conflict, labour relations, governance/fraud, HR fairness, harassment/retaliation, resource allocation, exit restrictions or organizational change?
2. **Search for primary records.** Court judgment/orders, statutory text, regulator notices, stock-exchange disclosures, official company statements, government inquiry records, union/collective agreements where public.
3. **Search two or more independent secondary sources** for contested events where possible. Compare dates, direct quotes, who is attributed, and whether the later report describes a final outcome.
4. **Search academic sources.** Prefer peer-reviewed studies, original research, university case studies, books from recognized academic publishers and open-access chapters. If only the abstract is available, do not infer findings beyond it.
5. **Search for the counter-perspective.** For management allegations, look for the company response and legal finding; for employee or union claims, look for management response and independent records. Avoid false equivalence: summarize evidence by its strength, not by mechanically giving every side equal weight.
6. **Check the final outcome.** A complaint, investigation, interim order, arbitration referral, settlement, conviction, acquittal and final appeal are different procedural states.
7. **Re-check current context.** If the user asks what is happening now or wants legal advice, use fresh official records and current reporting. Do not rely on an old casebook alone.

## 2. Evidence grading

- **A1 — Primary final finding:** final court judgment or final regulator decision, within the scope actually decided.
- **A2 — Primary procedural record:** interim order, notice, charge-sheet summary, investigation launch, filing or official record that does not finally determine merits.
- **B1 — Official/company statement:** reliable evidence of what the institution said or disclosed; not necessarily independent verification of the underlying claim.
- **B2 — Reputable reporting with attribution:** strong timeline and context, with quotes attributed to sources.
- **C1 — Academic research:** suitable for mechanisms, patterns and interpretation; record sample, method, period and limits.
- **C2 — Advocacy/worker or industry group report:** useful to understand a perspective or identify leads; cross-check contested claims.
- **D — Commentary / unverified social post:** not sufficient to establish a material fact; avoid using as the core of a case.

Evidence grade is not an overall “truth score.” A court order can be primary but procedural; reputable journalism can be excellent for a timeline but not a legal finding; an academic paper can be strong on a sample without predicting an individual.

## 3. Required case record schema

Store each case using these fields:

- Case ID and title
- Country/state, sector and organizational type
- Time period and dated key events
- Primary source(s) and source grade
- Secondary source(s) and source grade
- Parties / roles involved
- Verified events
- Attributed claims and responses
- Court/regulator findings and procedural status
- Formal power map and informal influence channels
- Underlying incentives / constraints
- Information asymmetry, voice, and process gaps
- Employees or third parties affected
- Decisions each actor realistically controlled
- Escalation signals and missed opportunities
- Transferable skill lessons
- Non-transferable/context-specific details
- Legal, ethical and privacy notes
- Last verified date

## 5. Corporate cases versus everyday workplace patterns

Public corporate cases disproportionately reveal severe disputes, major listed companies, regulators and litigation. Most ordinary employee politics (credit-taking, gatekeeping, favoritism, silent vetoes, manager alliances, performance-review ambiguity) will not be documented in a court judgment or credible newspaper. Use academic research and anonymized teaching cases to understand plausible mechanisms, but label them as generalized patterns or teaching cases. Never invent insider case details to fill gaps.

## 6. India-specific cautions

- “Lala company” is colloquial and not a formal category; use neutral observable dimensions instead of treating the phrase as a verdict.
- Family-owned businesses range from highly professionalized public groups to informal owner-managed enterprises; avoid overgeneralizing from one case.
- Labour relations may differ across direct employees, contract labour, apprentices, unions, gig workers and supervisory staff. Verify the applicable legal category.
- Language, hierarchy, caste, gender, class, region and family status can be relevant to a documented issue, but must not be used as shortcuts for predicting a person's motive or competence.
- Verify current Labour Code implementation, Central/State rules, POSH duties, Companies Act provisions and SEBI rules at the time of the answer. The package is not a substitute for legal advice.

## 7. Output checklist for research answers

- Lead with the most useful facts and identify the exact time period.
- Place citations next to claims.
- Separate confirmed facts, reported claims, formal findings and analysis.
- Identify what the source cannot establish.
- Include a timeline and stakeholder/power map when material.
- Explain mechanisms, not just personalities.
- Offer practical, ethical lessons and counterfactual prevention steps.
- State the source limitations and what requires further verification.
- Never predict the private motives, mental state or guilt of a person without sufficient documented evidence.
